Live-trading post-mortem · Aug 3 – Sep 8, 2026

I ran a trading bot for 500 real trades. The win rate was exactly 50.0%.

I built a strategy that shorts crypto pumps when they retrace, and ran it with real money on two exchanges for 37 days. Here are the results, and the analysis of why they came out the way they did.

What it traded

On 1-minute candles: detect a single-leg pump of 7% or more, then short at market the moment price retraces 100% back to where the pump started. Take-profit at 0.6× the pump size, stop-loss at 70% of the liquidation distance, and a forced exit after 30 minutes. A bot scanned ~700 symbols every 5 seconds and handled everything from signal to exit.

It ran on OKX and Bybit, starting at 9× leverage and dropping to 2× later on. Every figure here is exchange-confirmed P&L, fees and funding included.

Why 50% — the dissection

I didn't want to leave it at "bad luck," so I dug in from five directions.

1. The signal carried no directional information

Across 1,739 signals over three months, I measured the maximum move up and the maximum move down in the 30 minutes after entry. Medians: 2.96% and 3.08%. Essentially identical. Price went down further than up in 51% of cases. The signal wasn't selecting coins that would fall.

2. Winning and losing trades looked the same before entry

For 409 live trades, I pulled the 200 minutes of 1-minute candles before each entry and compared 18 features: pump size, speed, retrace depth, volatility, volume, taker buy/sell ratio. 17 of 18 showed no statistical difference. Overlay the volume curves and the winners and losers sit on top of each other right up to the entry line. They only diverge afterward — which is just the outcome re-read.

3. Twenty-four months of backtest said the same thing

Running the exact live configuration over 805 symbols and 24 months (560 million 1-minute candles): 15,733 trades, −$4,921, 44.5% win rate. I tried nine filters — market regime, 2-day momentum, buy ratio, time of day, and more. Every one of them cut losses; none of them created gains. The best subset never reached t = 1.

4. "Will it retrace 100%?" was predictable. It just wasn't profitable.

Tracking 52,199 pumps of 7%+, only 18.6% ever retraced fully. If you enter partway through the retrace at X%, the probability of completion is almost exactly X%: 32% at the 30% mark, 44% at 50%, 60% at 70%, 84% at 90%. The market had already priced the odds in.

Legs that retrace fast from the top complete 57% of the time; slow ones only 21%. But the group where this prediction works best had the worst P&L. Fast retraces mean big candles, which means your entry fill is already far past the level. The more predictable it was, the further price had already moved.

5. The strong start was the best two weeks in two years

The second week of live trading (Aug 10) made +$664 at a 57% win rate. Slicing the 24-month backtest into 78 weeks and ranking them, that week was #1 and the week before it was #2. The median week is −$9; 46% of weeks are positive. I switched the bot on between the two best weeks in two years, sized up on the confidence that gave me, and then the mean reverted.

What I learned

  1. Watch the t-statistic, not the win rate. A 50% win rate with a 1.0 profit factor has zero expectancy and loses the fees. A t of 0.05 after 500 trades doesn't mean "not sure yet" — it means "nothing there."
  2. The most dangerous moment is right after a good backtest goes live. If the backtest looked good because the most recent month was unusually good, then deployment day is exactly when the mean starts reverting.
  3. Predictable is not the same as profitable. "Will it complete the retrace?" was genuinely predictable. But the easier it was to predict, the worse the entry price had already become. Markets price probabilities before you can act on them.
  4. Filters select subsets; they don't create edge. Nine filters, and every one stopped at "lose less." Not one reached "make money."
  5. Position sizing kills the account before the strategy does. On the day I was most confident I put 44% of the account into a single trade, and that one day produced most of the 37-day loss. If the strategy is a coin flip, sizing is the outcome.

The bot is still running at $5 per trade — not to make money, but to keep watching for data that would change the conclusion. What 500 trades told me is less "the strategy is wrong" than "this signal contains no information," and it took real money and 37 days to find that out.
